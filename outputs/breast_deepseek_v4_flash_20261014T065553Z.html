      Why this scan: The candidate metabolite list is empty, so no CEST-detectable metabolite can be confirmed or refuted. This plan provides a generic breast CEST acquisition framework (axial slab through both breasts) that includes fibroglandular tissue (target), breast adipose tissue, and pectoral muscle. When future metabolite candidates are proposed, the ROI-differential design — comparing Z-spectra across tissues with known different metabolite concentrations — will distinguish real contrast from background MT/NOE.

      Scan protocol: 3 T, rectangular, B1=1 µT, 50 pulses × 20 ms (interpulse delay 10 ms, duty cycle 66.7%), total Tsat=1500 ms, TR=3000 ms

      SAR reasoning (the experimentalist agent's own; the pipeline applies no SAR model): At 3 T, a 66.7% duty-cycle, 1.0 µT B1, 1.5 s saturation train is well within IEC local SAR limits for breast (non-thoracic region, low SAR body area). Prone positioning and a dedicated breast coil further reduce local RF heating risk.

      Compromise rationale: No candidate metabolites were provided, so no compromise is needed. B1=1.0 µT and Tsat=1.5 s are standard default parameters for exploratory breast CEST at 3 T, providing moderate saturation across typical exchange regimes (0.5–3 kHz).

      Fat suppression: Water-selective excitation (a 1-2-1 binomial pulse or spectral-spatial excitation) is used for the readout. Standard chemical fat pre-saturation would inadvertently suppress CEST effects from overlapping fat and water signals; water-only excitation avoids this entirely and is standard in breast CEST MRI at 3 T.

      Motion mitigation: Breast MRI is performed with the patient prone, breasts suspended in a dedicated 16-channel bilateral breast coil. Respiratory motion of the breast is minimal; swallowing and bulk subject motion are the main concerns. A 'remain still, avoid swallowing' instruction with foam padding around the torso suffices. No respiratory or cardiac gating is needed. Scan time ~8 min.

      Slice coverage: Axial slab: centre at the nipple line (approximately T5–T6 vertebral level), 24 contiguous slices, 4 mm thickness, covering both breasts from the chest wall (anterior to pectoralis muscle) to the nipple. Field of view 320 × 320 mm, 128 × 128 matrix for Z-spectrum readout. The slab includes fibroglandular tissue, breast adipose tissue, and pectoral muscle.

      Structures in FOV: breast fibroglandular tissue, breast adipose tissue, pectoral muscle, subcutaneous fat, skin

      Differential analysis plan: With no candidate metabolites, no differential analysis is performed. The framework is ready: for any future candidate at offset X ppm, (1) extract B0-corrected Z-spectra per ROI per subject, (2) compute MTRasym = Z(-X) - Z(+X), (3) compute the mean MTRasym in fibroglandular vs adipose vs muscle, (4) test with paired ANOVA across subjects. A significant ROI effect that tracks the known tissue concentration gradient (e.g., high creatine in muscle, low in fat) supports a real metabolite contribution.

      Not actively targeted: The candidate metabolites list was empty. No metabolites were excluded; none were present to design around.

      Caveats: This scan uses a single B1 (1.0 µT) that may be suboptimal for fast-exchange or slow-exchange metabolites in future studies. Breast MRI at 3 T suffers from B0 inhomogeneity at skin-air and chest-wall interfaces that may persist after WASSR correction. Partial-volume effects between fibroglandular tissue and adipose are unavoidable in small-breasted subjects given 4 mm slices. The lack of a candidate list means the scan cannot yet confirm or refute any particular metabolite; it provides a reusable acquisition framework.
